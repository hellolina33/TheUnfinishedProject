Why I built it

The 2 pm slump is real. Slides won’t fix it.

For a company summit, I had to bring a room back to life right after lunch. I turned 12 senior leaders into animated characters and had them lead 10 silly moves on the big screen: jazz hands, a robot malfunction, air guitar, a freeze.

It landed well with the team. The next question was obvious: could any facilitator make one for their own session, without a designer?

That animation took a lot of custom work. Energizer Studio packs it into a tool anyone can use, with their own team, their own words and their own colours.

1

Energizers feel generic

Stock videos and “stand up and stretch” slides get polite smiles. Seeing your own leaders or colleagues on screen gets people moving.

2

Facilitators lose time

Timing slides, finding a countdown, syncing music, fixing fonts on the room laptop. Here, the show runs by itself.

3

Tools need accounts and Wi-Fi

No sign-up, no licence, no upload. The file opens in Chrome, even with the network down.

What you can build

Three formats, ready to run.

Start from a template, then make it yours. Every scene has its own duration, so a 90-second energizer and a 10-minute icebreaker use the same tool.

Movement energizer · 1.5 to 3 min

Copy the leader

Your colleagues’ videos lead each move, one or two at a time. A ring counts down, a sticker adds the joke, a “FREEZE!” banner lands on cue.

Icebreaker · 2 to 10 min

Talk to someone new

One big question per screen, a talk timer and three beeps before the swap. Works for onboarding, workshops, kick-offs and offsites.

Breaks & mixes · any length

Your own running order

Mix moves, questions and timed breaks. Add a rules screen, a 3-2-1 countdown and an outro that thanks the room.

Opener

Your title and up to three featured videos.

Meet the crew

Every video at once, as playing cards.

Up to three rules, one underlined, and a bottom line.

3-2-1 Go

Countdown with beeps and a “Let’s move!” burst.

Timer

A full-screen countdown for breaks or pair work.

Outro

Thank-you title, the whole crew taking a bow, confetti.

How it works

From blank to big screen in four steps.

Everything happens in your browser. Close the tab and your edits are still there next time.

1

Pick a template

Movement energizer, icebreaker questions or blank. Add, reorder, duplicate or delete scenes.

2

Drop your videos

Drag in short MP4s. Set start and end to skip still frames, then zoom to frame each person. Videos are assigned to moves automatically.

3

Write and time it

Change every text, duration and colour with a live preview. Add your own music if you have the rights.

4

Export and present

“Export show” saves one HTML file with everything inside. Open it in the room and press Start.

What’s inside

Built for the room, not for a demo.

Runs by itself

Transitions, countdowns, beeps and music are timed for you. You can stay with your group instead of the laptop.

Projector-proof

Designed at 1920 × 1080 with huge type. Fonts are embedded, so nothing breaks on a borrowed laptop.

One file to share

The exported show carries its videos, music and settings. Email it, put it on a drive, open it offline.

Editable later

Anyone who receives the file can press Edit, change a word, and export a new version.

Your brand

Five colours, ink and paper, a corner label and a tag on the video cards. Change them once, the whole show follows.

Smart text

Type {i}, {total} or {mins} and the numbers stay right when you add or remove scenes. Double asterisks make a word bold.

Private by design

No server, no account, no tracking of your content. Edits are saved in your own browser only.

Inclusive by default

The templates invite people to join from their seat, and every move can be done in a small space.

Remote-friendly

Share the browser tab in Teams, Zoom or Meet, with sound on, and your remote crowd joins in.

Facilitator tips

Make it land.

  • ✓
    Short clips work best.About 10 seconds of 720p per person. Ask colleagues to film themselves against a plain wall, full body, phone in landscape.
  • ✓
    Keep moves gentle.Feet grounded, hands close, nothing that needs space or a warm-up. Always say “or join from your seat”.
  • ✓
    Say the magic sentence.“Out of sync is perfect.” People move more when they know nobody is grading them.
  • ✓
    Two minutes is enough.Eight to ten moves of 8 seconds, or three to five questions. Stop while the room still wants more.
  • ✓
    Test on the room laptop.Open the file, press Start, check full screen and sound. Takes one minute and saves the moment.
  • ✓
    Lead by example.Do the first move with them. When the facilitator commits, the room follows.
i
Ask before you use someone’s face. The exported file contains the videos you add. Get people’s OK before putting them on screen, and only add music you have the rights to play. Animated avatars of your colleagues work too, as long as they agreed.

Q&A

Questions people ask.

Is it really free? Do I need an account?

Yes, free, and no account. Open the studio, build, export. It is Day 7 of The Unfinished 30, a challenge where I ship one free prototype every day.

Where do my videos go?

Nowhere. They stay on your computer. The studio runs in your browser and saves your work in the browser’s own storage. When you export, the videos are packed into the HTML file you download.

Where do I get the videos?

Ask colleagues to film themselves for about 10 seconds doing one move, against a plain background. You can also use animated characters of your team, made with the AI video tool you already use, as long as each person agreed.

No videos at all? The icebreaker template works with text only.

Which video formats work? How big will the file be?

MP4 (H.264) is the safest. WebM works in Chrome too, and MOV files usually do if they were recorded in H.264. The file grows with your videos: twelve 10-second clips in 720p make a file of roughly 15 to 20 MB. The studio does not compress videos yet, so shorter, smaller clips are better.

Does it work offline, on Mac, on Windows?

Yes, offline: everything is inside the file. It is tested in Chrome. Edge uses the same engine and should behave the same, but Safari and Firefox are not tested yet.

Can I use it in Teams, Zoom or Meet?

Yes. Open the file in Chrome, then share that tab or window with “share sound” on. People at home follow along on their camera.

Can I put it inside PowerPoint or Google Slides?

Not directly. Keep the HTML file open in a browser tab next to your deck and switch to it when it’s time. Pressing F makes it full screen.

Can I change the length of each part?

Yes. Every scene has its own duration in seconds. The timeline and the “Under 2 minutes” style texts update when you use the {mins} token.

Can someone else edit the show I send them?

Yes. They open the file, press E or click Edit, make their changes and export a new file. The original stays untouched.

Can I add music?

Yes, any MP3 you have the rights to play. You choose when it starts, its volume and whether it loops. No music is included.

Can I write in another language?

Yes, every text on screen is yours to write, in any language that uses Latin letters. The editor itself is in English.

Can I use it at work?

Yes. It is free to use for your sessions. Check your company’s rules on employee images and music before you share a show widely.

Honest note

What’s still unfinished.

  • Browsers:tested in Chrome only. Edge should work the same way; Safari and Firefox are untested.
  • File size:videos are embedded as they are, with no compression, so big clips make big files.
  • Scene types:no live poll, no “would you rather”, no team scoreboard yet.
  • Phones:you can watch a show on a phone, but editing is built for a laptop.
  • Editor language:the editor is in English only for now.
