90-Minute Focus Cycles

Matches demanding work to a rhythm many people notice in their own attention, with a real break at each end.

Expected result

A work-rest rhythm informed by your own attention log.

Fictional worked case

An analyst notices attention dropping late in the morning and wants to test a work-rest rhythm.

Try a longer block for analysis and writing. If concentration drops earlier during writing, shorten that block while keeping the analysis block.

Try the same rhythm for data analysis and writing. Observe whether attention drops earlier in either task.

Steps

  1. Work in 90-minute blocks on demanding work.
  2. Take a real break after each: stand, walk, do not check messages.
  3. Schedule the hardest work for your usual peak hours.

Review

For five days, record when attention drops and adjust the next block to match the observation.

Limitations

Treat the suggested intervals as starting points and adjust for the task and your energy.

Nathaniel Kleitman, rest-activity cycle research (1950s to 1960s). The 90-minute block is a popular extension, not a settled finding.
