Second-Order Thinking

Looks past the immediate result to what that result will cause next.

Expected result

A map of knock-on effects and an early warning indicator.

Fictional worked case

Support can speed up replies, but easier access may increase demand and create a new backlog.

Faster replies improve the first interaction. They may bring more requests, which can grow the queue and erase the gain. Monitor both measures.

Faster replies may attract more requests. Watch the size of the queue after changing the response process.

Steps

  1. Write the decision and its immediate effect (first order).
  2. Ask 'and then what?' for the next two effects.
  3. Decide only after you have looked at the second-order effects.

Review

Monitor both reply time and queue size for two weeks.

Limitations

Write a mechanism between each pair of effects. Mark links that are still hypotheses.
