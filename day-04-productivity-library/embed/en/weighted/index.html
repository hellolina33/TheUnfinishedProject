Decision Matrix

Compares options against criteria you weight yourself, so the trade-offs are written down, not felt.

Expected result

A ranked comparison and a sensitivity check.

Fictional worked case

Three vendors vary in cost, timing and maintenance. The team wants weights agreed before seeing the totals.

Option C scores 0.40 × 4 + 0.35 × 3 + 0.25 × 5 = 3.90. A and B each score 3.70. A small lead makes the timing weight worth testing.

Use a 1–5 scale. A and B score 3.70 and C scores 3.90. Test the weights before choosing C.

Steps

  1. List the criteria that matter: cost, time, fit, and so on.
  2. Give each criterion a weight from 1 to 5.
  3. Score each option from 1 to 5 per criterion, multiply by the weight, and add up the totals.

Review

Change the timing weight and check whether vendor C remains first.

Limitations

Choose weights before scoring and use the same scoring direction for all criteria.

Pugh matrix, Stuart Pugh (engineering design, 1980s to 1990s). Weighted scoring is its common generalisation.
