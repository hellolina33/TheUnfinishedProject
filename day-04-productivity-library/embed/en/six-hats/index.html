Six Thinking Hats

Gives a group one way of thinking at a time, so critics and optimists stop talking over each other.

Expected result

A decision that records facts, risks and a proposed experiment.

Fictional worked case

A team is debating a new supplier workflow. Facts, workload fears and pilot ideas are mixed together.

Twelve requests are a fact. Concern about workload is a reaction. Supplier delay is a risk. A limited pilot is an idea that can be tested against those inputs.

Keep the supplier delay separate from concerns about workload. End by deciding whether to run a pilot.

Steps

  1. Write the decision on a page.
  2. Spend five minutes on each hat: facts, feelings, risks, benefits, ideas, process.
  3. Decide after the blue hat, not before.

Review

Write a pilot decision with one risk to monitor and one benefit to measure.

Limitations

Give everyone time in each mode. Assigning permanent hats to people loses perspectives.

Edward de Bono, Six Thinking Hats (1985).
