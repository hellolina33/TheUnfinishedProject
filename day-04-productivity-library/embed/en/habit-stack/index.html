Habit Stacking

Attaches a new habit to one you already have, so you do not need to remember to start it.

Expected result

One new habit attached to an existing action.

Fictional worked case

A planner forgets to choose a priority before the workday starts but makes coffee every morning.

Coffee already happens. Attach one line about today’s priority to the end of that routine, then put it on the calendar.

Attach a short planning gesture to coffee, a routine that already happens.

Steps

  1. Pick something you do every day without thinking: coffee, brushing your teeth.
  2. Use the form: 'After I [existing habit], I will [new habit].'
  3. Keep the new habit tiny, under two minutes, at first.

Review

After five mornings, count how often the priority made it into the calendar.

Limitations

Start with a small action that fits immediately after the cue.

Popularised by BJ Fogg, Tiny Habits (2019), and James Clear, Atomic Habits (2018). No single inventor.
