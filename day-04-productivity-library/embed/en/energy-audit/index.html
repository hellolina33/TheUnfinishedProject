Energy Audit

Maps which activities drain your energy and which restore it, not just how long they take.

Expected result

A schedule adjustment based on observed energy patterns.

Fictional worked case

Writing feels easy in the morning but status meetings and expense admin drain energy later.

Morning writing tends to recharge attention. Group expenses and shorten status meetings for a week, then check whether afternoons feel less draining.

Protect morning writing. Batch expenses and check whether the change reduces end-of-day fatigue.

Steps

  1. For two weeks, note each activity and your energy afterwards: up, flat or down.
  2. Place each activity on the grid by drain and gain.
  3. Protect the work in the top left. Cut or fix the work in the bottom right.

Review

Compare afternoon energy for one week after batching expenses and shortening meetings.

Limitations

Log energy before and after comparable tasks. Avoid drawing conclusions from a single unusually busy day.

Original studio exercise inspired by energy-management themes in Jim Loehr and Tony Schwartz, The Power of Full Engagement (2003). The matrix is an adaptation created for this guide.
