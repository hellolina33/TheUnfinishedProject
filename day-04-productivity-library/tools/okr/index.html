OKRs

Define one qualitative objective and a few measurable results, separate from the tasks used to reach them.

Expected result

An objective with measurable outcomes and a review rhythm.

Fictional worked case

New users often leave before their first task. The team needs measures that separate activation, speed and drop-off.

Activation can rise while users still take too long to start. Tracking activation, first-task time and drop-off together exposes those different failure points.

Track activation, time to first task and drop-off together to see where onboarding improves.

Steps

  1. Write an inspiring but precise objective.
  2. Add two to four measurable key results.
  3. Check progress regularly and adjust initiatives.

Review

Review all three measures weekly on the same cohort definition.

Limitations

Keep work items such as “launch a tutorial” separate from results such as “reduce time to first task.”

Andy Grove's Intel management practices; John Doerr, Measure What Matters (2018).
