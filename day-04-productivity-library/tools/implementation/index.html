Implementation Intentions

Turn an intention into an if–then response triggered by a specific situation.

Expected result

A short if–then rule with an observable trigger.

Fictional worked case

An urgent message often triggers a rushed yes. The team wants a short check before accepting new work.

The urgent request is the cue. A two-question impact check happens before replying. Test the sequence on the next five requests.

The urgent request is the trigger. Checking its impact is the action to rehearse.

Steps

  1. Identify the situation where you usually get stuck.
  2. Write an if–then action that is under your control.
  3. Use the cue and improve the plan if needed.

Review

Count how often the impact check happens on the next five urgent requests.

Limitations

A trigger like “when I have time” is hard to notice. Choose an event you can recognise as it happens.

Peter Gollwitzer, implementation-intention research (1990s).
