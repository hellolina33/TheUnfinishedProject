Maker’s vs Manager’s Schedule

Explains why one meeting in the middle of an afternoon can cost a whole block of focused work.

Expected result

A weekly calendar with long creation blocks and grouped meetings.

Fictional worked case

A designer loses every morning to meetings, although a mock-up needs two uninterrupted hours.

Put mock-up work in an uninterrupted morning. Batch supplier calls and scope discussions later so coordination takes one part of the day.

Draft the mock-up in the uninterrupted morning. Batch coordination into later slots.

Steps

  1. Decide whether your work needs long blocks (maker) or short, frequent slots (manager).
  2. Batch the maker work into blocks with no meetings.
  3. Keep meetings short and clustered, in the afternoon.

Review

After a week, compare uninterrupted production time and note any coordination delays.

Limitations

Confirm which meetings genuinely need your presence before grouping everything.

Paul Graham, Maker’s Schedule, Manager’s Schedule (essay, 2009).
