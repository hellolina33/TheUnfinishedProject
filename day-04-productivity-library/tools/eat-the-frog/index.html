Eat the Frog

Starts the day with the task you would most like to put off, while your attention is still fresh.

Expected result

A first draft or another visible piece of the hardest task.

Fictional worked case

A report is due tomorrow. The introduction has slipped twice while the author answers easy emails.

The introduction has stalled the whole report. Forty minutes is enough to write a first version a colleague can read. Email can move to the next block.

Before opening email, finish one visible piece of the report: the introduction.

Steps

  1. Each evening, choose the task you are most likely to avoid tomorrow.
  2. Do it first thing, before email.
  3. Then work through the rest of the list.

Review

Open the draft at 9 am and finish an introduction a colleague could review at 9:40.

Limitations

Choose a task you are avoiding that also advances a real priority. Difficulty alone is a poor criterion.

Brian Tracy, Eat That Frog! (2001). The phrase is also attributed to Mark Twain; that attribution is not verified.
